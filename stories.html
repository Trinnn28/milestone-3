Stories: Rivalries, Dynasties, Historic Seasons & Expansion

Explore key moments, dynasties, rivalries, record-breakers, and expansion milestones across the NBA (1946–2026) and WNBA (1997–2026). Because the two leagues have different founding eras and season lengths (82 games in the NBA vs. up to 44 games in the WNBA), their statistical histories are presented side by side on separate scales.

Chart note: Statistics current through the 2025–26 NBA Finals (June 2026) and 2026 WNBA Finals (Fall 2026). Hover over any bar for exact championship years and MVP winners who won the award while playing for that franchise.

1. Celtics vs. Lakers: Basketball’s Defining Championship Rivalry (NBA)

No rivalry in North American basketball matches the Boston Celtics (18 NBA championships) and Los Angeles Lakers (17 NBA championships), who have met 12 times in the NBA Finals and account for nearly half of all titles since 1947.

  • 1959–1969 (Russell vs. West & Baylor): Bill Russell—who won 5 NBA MVPs while playing for Boston—led the Celtics past the Lakers in seven Finals series during an 11-title dynasty.
  • 1980s Revival (Bird vs. Magic): Larry Bird (3 Celtics MVPs, 1984–86) and Magic Johnson (3 Lakers MVPs, 1987, 1989, 1990) met in the 1984, 1985, and 1987 NBA Finals, transforming the NBA’s television and cultural reach.
  • Modern Chapters (2008–2024): Boston won in 2008 behind Paul Pierce and Kevin Garnett (who won his 2004 MVP earlier with Minnesota), Kobe Bryant (2008 Lakers MVP) answered in Game 7 of the 2010 Finals, LeBron James tied Boston at 17 banners in 2020, and Jayson Tatum and Jaylen Brown lifted Banner 18 in 2024.

2. WNBA Dynasties: From the 4-Peat Comets to A’ja Wilson’s Aces—and Houston’s 2027 Return (WNBA)

Across the WNBA’s first 30 seasons (1997–2026), four franchises have set the championship standard—and the original dynasty is officially coming back:

  • Houston Comets (4 Titles: 1997, 1998, 1999, 2000): Led by Cynthia Cooper (back-to-back WNBA MVPs in 1997 & 1998, plus four straight Finals MVPs), Sheryl Swoopes (3 WNBA MVPs with Houston in 2000, 2002, & 2005), and Tina Thompson, Houston achieved the only four-peat in WNBA history. Following the 2026 season, the WNBA approved relocating the Connecticut Sun to Houston to revive the Houston Comets beginning in 2027.
  • Minnesota Lynx (4 Titles: 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017) & Seattle Storm (4 Titles: 2004, 2010, 2018, 2020): Cheryl Reeve’s Lynx dominated odd-numbered years in the 2010s with Maya Moore (2014 MVP) and Sylvia Fowles (2017 MVP), while Seattle went a perfect 4–0 in Finals appearances behind Sue Bird, Lauren Jackson (3 Storm MVPs), and Breanna Stewart (2018 Storm MVP; later 2023 Liberty MVP).
  • Las Vegas Aces (3 Titles: 2022, 2023, 2025): Coached by Becky Hammon, Las Vegas captured three championships in four years anchored by A’ja Wilson, who set an all-time WNBA record by winning her fifth regular-season MVP award in 2026 (2020, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026).

3. Chasing Perfection: 72–10 Chicago Bulls & 73–9 Golden State Warriors (NBA)

Two 82-game NBA regular seasons stand above every other in win-loss history:

  • 1995–96 Chicago Bulls (72–10 Regular Season, 15–3 Playoffs): Returning for his first full season after 1993–94 retirement, Michael Jordan won his fourth of 5 NBA MVPs with Chicago, led the league in scoring (30.4 PPG), and teamed with Scottie Pippen and Dennis Rodman to win the first title of Chicago’s second three-peat (1996–1998).
  • 2015–16 Golden State Warriors (73–9 Regular Season): Stephen Curry shattered his own NBA single-season record with 402 three-pointers during the 2015–16 regular season and became the first unanimous MVP in NBA history, pushing Cleveland to a legendary Game 7 in the 2016 Finals (where LeBron James and Kyrie Irving completed the only 3–1 Finals comeback in NBA history) before Golden State added titles in 2017, 2018, and 2022.

4. Historic 2025 & 2026 Breakthroughs: Valkyries, Knicks, and Thunder (NBA & WNBA)

The 2025 and 2026 seasons delivered historic championship stories across both leagues:

  • Golden State Valkyries Win First Game 7 in WNBA Finals History (2026 WNBA Season): Just one year after debuting as a 2025 expansion franchise in San Francisco, coach Natalie Nakase’s Valkyries defeated the Atlanta Dream 4–3 in the 2026 WNBA Finals—the first Finals Game 7 ever played under the WNBA’s expanded best-of-seven format—with Gabby Williams earning Finals MVP honors.
  • New York Knicks End a 53-Year Title Drought (2025–26 NBA Season): For the first time since 1973, Madison Square Garden celebrated an NBA championship in June 2026 as Finals MVP Jalen Brunson, OG Anunoby, Mikal Bridges, Josh Hart, and Karl-Anthony Towns defeated Victor Wembanyama and the San Antonio Spurs 4–1.
  • Oklahoma City Thunder & Back-to-Back MVP Shai Gilgeous-Alexander (2024–25 & 2025–26 NBA Seasons): One year earlier, Oklahoma City won Game 7 of the 2025 NBA Finals over the Indiana Pacers behind Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, who went on to win consecutive regular-season NBA MVP awards in 2024–25 and 2025–26.

5. All-Time Statistical Icons—And Why League Context Matters (NBA & WNBA)

Comparing career totals requires respecting each league’s schedule structure: NBA seasons have featured 82 regular-season games since 1967–68, whereas WNBA seasons grew gradually from 28 games (1997) to 34 games (2003–2019), 40 games (2023–2024), and 44 games (2025–2026).

  • WNBA Career Benchmarks (1997–2026): Diana Taurasi (Phoenix Mercury, 2004–2024) retired as the only player in WNBA history past 10,000 regular-season points (10,646 PTS). Sue Bird (Seattle Storm) holds the WNBA all-time assists record (3,234 AST), and Tamika Catchings (Indiana Fever, 2011 MVP) leads WNBA history in career steals (1,074 STL). Meanwhile, Caitlin Clark reshaped single-season playmaking benchmarks with Indiana while helping drive record arena attendance across the league.
  • NBA Career Benchmarks (1946–2026): LeBron James surpassed Kareem Abdul-Jabbar’s long-standing 38,387-point mark to pass 40,000+ regular-season NBA points (winning 4 NBA MVPs across Cleveland and Miami before winning the 2020 title with the Lakers), while John Stockton still holds the NBA’s career assists (15,806 AST) and steals (3,265 STL) records for the Utah Jazz.

6. Redrawing the Basketball Map: The 2025–2030 Expansion Wave (NBA & WNBA)

Professional basketball’s North American map is expanding faster in the late 2020s than at any point in decades:

  • WNBA Expansion from 12 to 18 Teams (2025–2030): Following the Golden State Valkyries (2025), the WNBA launched two 30th-anniversary expansion franchises in 2026—the Portland Fire and Canada’s first WNBA team, the Toronto Tempo—and officially awarded three additional franchises backed by NBA ownership groups: Cleveland (2028), Detroit (2029), and Philadelphia (2030).
  • NBA Expansion Evaluations in Seattle & Las Vegas (Target 2028–29): In March 2026, the NBA Board of Governors voted 30–0 to formally advance expansion evaluations for Seattle (poised to revive the beloved SuperSonics tradition at Climate Pledge Arena) and Las Vegas (already home to NBA Summer League and the Emirates NBA Cup Final Four).
